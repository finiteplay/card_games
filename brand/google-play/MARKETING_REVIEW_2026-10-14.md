# FinitePlay Google Play marketing review — 2026-10-14

Recommendation: present familiar card games people can settle into, with helpful tools and
the explicit promise of no ads, no purchases, and entirely local play. Lead each listing
with its own game experience, then explain the family promise immediately. Keep the virtual
chip disclosures prominent for Blackjack and Hold'em.

This is research and proposed copy, not an applied listing update. The existing `listing.md`
files remain the local record of previously saved copy. No Console fields, releases, country
settings, or review submissions were changed during this review.

## Market positioning

The sampled competitors already advertise offline play, hints, winnable deals, and relaxation.
Those features alone will not make FinitePlay distinctive. Several also display ads and
in-app purchases. In the sampled FreeCell reviews, users complained about intrusive ads and
offline ad-removal checks. These are directional examples, not representative survey results.
See [MobilityWare Solitaire](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobilityware.solitaire),
[Spider](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobilityware.spider), and
[FreeCell](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mobilityware.freecell).

For poker, [Poker Offline: Texas Holdem](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.redrocket.poker)
combines offline tournaments with bonuses, ads, and purchases. For Blackjack,
[BlackJack: 21 Card Game](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.gsss.blackjack)
combines offline dealer play and basic-strategy hints with challenges and monetization.
[Blackjack Strategy Trainer](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=software.counter.blackjack)
also promotes offline privacy: FinitePlay cannot claim that combination is unique.

The opportunity is a coherent, credible package: recognizable games, readable tables, useful
decision tools, and no monetization interruptions. Avoid trying to win on jackpots, daily
rewards, streak pressure, or claims of superior intelligence. Avoid unsupported brain-health
claims, casino-profit promises, and claims that simulated success predicts real-money success.

| Game | Primary player need | Main benefit to communicate | Supporting proof to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Klondike | Familiar solitaire during a break | Choose draw 1 or 3 and a suitable challenge | Actual board, difficulty choices, optional hint |
| Spider | A longer sequence-building puzzle | Choose 1, 2, or 4 suits | Completed run and actual suit selection |
| FreeCell | A puzzle driven by planning | Use visible cards, free cells, undo and hints | A meaningful multi-card move, then hint/undo |
| Blackjack | Enjoy dealer play and practice decisions | Virtual chips and optional basic-strategy hints | Player decision versus dealer up card; an actual hint |
| Hold'em | A complete local poker tournament | Five computer opponents and rising blinds | Active six-seat table, betting choice, tournament result |

Keep the family identity and existing icon style. Replace generic evaluative language such as
"quality games" with concrete facts and visible gameplay. Translate "certified" into the player
benefit "starts with a solution"; keep the scope and hint limitations clear in the full copy.

## Titles and short descriptions to consider

These proposed titles retain FinitePlay while adding recognizable game terms. Adding
"Solitaire" to Klondike/Spider/FreeCell and "Poker" to Hold'em should clarify the product;
there is no evidence here for a particular search-ranking improvement. This is a proposed
revision to the current `<Game> by FinitePlay` naming guideline, not an approved rename.

| Game | Proposed title | Characters / 30 | Proposed short description | Characters / 80 |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| Klondike | Klondike Solitaire: FinitePlay | 30 | Classic solitaire with solvable deals, draw 1 or 3, and offline hints | 69 |
| Spider | Spider Solitaire: FinitePlay | 28 | Play 1, 2 or 4 suits offline with solvable deals and optional hints | 67 |
| FreeCell | FreeCell Solitaire: FinitePlay | 30 | Plan each move with solvable deals, offline hints, undo and replay | 66 |
| Blackjack | Blackjack 21: FinitePlay | 24 | Play blackjack offline with virtual chips and optional basic-strategy hints | 75 |
| Hold'em | Texas Holdem Poker: FinitePlay | 30 | Offline Texas Hold’em tournaments with virtual chips and five computer opponents | 80 |

Holdem in the proposed title omits the apostrophe to fit the brand and Poker within the limit;
the description retains the conventional Hold'em spelling. Keeping the current title is also
reasonable if that typography is preferred. Put the complete phrase Texas Hold'em poker
naturally in the full description, rather than repeating keyword variations.

The casino short descriptions currently spend most space on exclusions. Move that list into
the first full-description paragraph, retaining its prominence while making room for gameplay.
The warning does not identify a triggering word; Spider has it without mentioning ads or price.
Do not claim the replacements clear the warning until Console validates them.

Google limits titles to 30 characters and short descriptions to 80. Its guidance favors clear
functionality over promotional language, redundant keywords, and price claims. See
[metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842) and
[preview asset guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en).

## Proposed full descriptions

These deliberately omit catalog and locale counts that can age. Validate the actual uploaded
build before applying any feature claim. No guarantee of a win is implied by a solvable initial
deal or a strategy hint.

### Klondike

Play classic Klondike Solitaire offline, with draw-one and draw-three modes, a choice of
difficulty, and hints when you want help. No ads, in-app purchases, subscriptions, accounts,
or tracking. Your progress and statistics stay on your device.

Choose a comfortable challenge or work toward a tougher puzzle. Deals from Trivial through
Expert start with a verified solution. The separate Insane level contains uncertified deals.
Your choices still matter: a solvable deal can lead to a dead end.

Features:
- Draw one or draw three
- Six difficulty levels
- Tap or drag cards, undo a move, or replay a deal
- Optional hints based on the board you are playing
- Automatic finish and optional foundation moves
- Light and dark themes, sound and animation controls
- On-device statistics and personal-best comparisons

Hints look for a path to a win from your current position. When the search cannot establish
one within its limit, it reports that result rather than guaranteeing a winning move.

FinitePlay makes card games that respect your time, privacy, and attention.

### Spider

Build complete King-to-Ace runs in Spider Solitaire, with one, two, or four suits and optional
hints. The full game works offline. No ads, in-app purchases, subscriptions, accounts, or
tracking. Your progress and statistics stay on your device.

Start with one suit or take on the extra planning of two and four suits. Every included deal
starts with a verified solution, so there is a way to clear the initial board. Your decisions
still determine whether you reach it.

Features:
- One-, two-, and four-suit games
- Tap cards or drag same-suit sequences
- Undo moves and replay deals
- Legal-move highlighting and optional hints
- Light and dark themes, sound and animation controls
- On-device statistics

Guided hints can follow a known solution. After you take a different path, finding a new
solution is subject to search limits; hints are not a promise that every position can be won.

FinitePlay makes card games that respect your time, privacy, and attention.

### FreeCell

Plan your moves in classic FreeCell Solitaire, with every card visible, four free cells, and
optional hints. Play entirely offline. No ads, in-app purchases, subscriptions, accounts, or
tracking. Your progress and statistics stay on your device.

Each included deal starts with a verified solution. Use free cells and empty columns to make
space, move card sequences, and work toward the foundations. Undo or replay a deal to explore
a different approach.

Features:
- Solvable starting deals
- Tap or drag cards and legal multi-card sequences
- Undo, replay, and automatic finish
- Optional hints based on the current board
- Optional automatic foundation moves
- Light and dark themes, sound and animation controls
- On-device statistics and personal-best comparisons

A solvable starting deal does not guarantee every later position is winnable. Hints search
for a path from your current board and report when the search cannot establish one.

FinitePlay makes card games that respect your time, privacy, and attention.

### Blackjack

Play blackjack offline against a computer dealer, with virtual chips and optional
basic-strategy hints. No ads or in-app purchases. No accounts, tracking, online play, or matches
against real people. Your progress and statistics stay on your device.

Choose your bet, play your hand, and decide when to hit, stand, double, or split. Ask for a
basic-strategy hint when you want guidance, then follow the dealer as the round settles.

Features:
- Hit, stand, double, and split
- Insurance, dealer peek, and 3:2 blackjack payouts
- Optional basic-strategy hints
- Virtual bankroll and on-device statistics
- Entirely local play against a computer dealer

This is simulated blackjack for adults aged 18 and over. Chips have no monetary value.
There are no real-money bets, deposits, purchases, cash-outs, or prizes. Strategy hints do not
guarantee winning hands or real-money results.

FinitePlay makes card games that respect your time, privacy, and attention.

### Texas Hold'em

Play a six-seat Texas Hold'em poker tournament offline against five computer opponents,
using virtual chips. No ads or in-app purchases. No accounts, tracking, online play, or matches
against real people. Your tournament progress and statistics stay on your device.

Everyone starts with the same stack. As the blinds rise, choose when to fold, call, raise, or
go all-in and aim to be the last player at the table. Save your tournament and return to it later.

Features:
- No-limit Texas Hold'em against five computer playing styles
- Fold, check, call, bet, raise, and go all-in
- Bet-sizing controls and side pots
- Optional hints with estimated equity and pot odds
- Saved tournaments and on-device statistics
- Rules, hand rankings, and strategy help
- Portrait and landscape layouts
- Theme, sound, animation, and language settings

This is simulated poker for adults aged 18 and over. Chips have no monetary value.
There are no real-money bets, deposits, purchases, cash-outs, or prizes. Hint estimates and
simulated results do not guarantee winning hands or real-money results.

FinitePlay makes card games that respect your time, privacy, and attention.

## Screenshot and visual plan

Fix ordering before redesigning icons. Four listings currently lead with settings; that hides
the product's main experience. Retain recognizable family icons and use actual release gameplay
as the visual proof. Generic card art alone does not communicate the distinctive tools.

| Position | Solitaires | Blackjack | Hold'em |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Readable game in progress | A meaningful player decision versus dealer | Active six-seat tournament table |
| 2 | Actual hint or useful move | A real basic-strategy hint | Actual bet sizing or equity/pot-odds hint |
| 3 | A different meaningful gameplay state | Split hand or settled round | Showdown, side pot, or tournament result |
| 4 | Theme/variation demonstrated through gameplay | Another real gameplay state/theme | Another real gameplay state/orientation |
| 5 | Settings, if they add useful information | Settings, if useful | Settings, if useful |

Aim for four or five useful screenshots, including three that show gameplay. Klondike and
Spider have only two gameplay captures among their three images; Hold'em has one among two.
They need additional gameplay captures, not merely rearrangement. Inspect Blackjack's four
generic-named assets visually before deciding which to reuse.

Google distinguishes the two-image publishing minimum from its three suitably sized gameplay
images guidance for games in some recommendation formats. Settings captures do not communicate
gameplay. The Console also displays a four-image promotion prompt. Use at least four total and
three gameplay captures at 1080x1920 or 1920x1080; that addresses both observed instructions.
Keep captions brief and subordinate to the actual UI. Do not put price claims or "best" badges
on icons/feature art. See [Google's asset guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en).

Possible functional captions: "Draw one or three", "Choose 1, 2 or 4 suits", "Plan with four
free cells", "Optional strategy hints", "Five computer opponents". Keep the no-ads/purchases
statement in the opening full description and verify Data safety matches the shipped app.

After the still-image work, consider a 15–25 second real-gameplay video. Show play immediately,
then one helpful feature and another gameplay state. A long logo introduction would spend the
viewer’s attention before showing the game. Producing a video is lower priority than fixing
the existing first screenshots and warnings.

## Update sequence and measurement

1. Preserve an en-US before-copy snapshot and the existing screenshot order. Verify production
   versus tester availability separately; the account currently lists Closed testing for all five.
   Resolve release readiness before spending on broad public acquisition.
2. Validate the proposed short descriptions in Console. Clear the three observed warnings or
   obtain a specific explanation; do not assume a cosmetic rewrite will fix every flag.
3. Reorder gameplay first and capture the missing gameplay images. Put settings last.
4. Choose whether to adopt the proposed titles. Update the full descriptions with the approved
   benefit-first copy while retaining the early no-ads/no-purchases and virtual-chip disclosures.
5. Save the approved versions locally and upload through the existing publishing workflow.
   Review the exact diff and submission status. Do not alter audience, country, or content-rating
   choices as a marketing optimization.
6. Add reviewed store translations in a small number of languages chosen from actual visitor
   demand and review capacity. Do not equate translated interface strings with localized listings,
   and do not default to translating into 30 languages before learning which audiences arrive.
7. Establish visitors, acquisitions, and conversion by game, traffic source, country, and language.
   Compare like sources; a shift toward warmer tester traffic can look like a copy improvement.
8. When traffic supports it, test one variable against the baseline: first screenshot, then short
   description, then icon if needed. For text, compare a familiar-play message with a
   guidance/planning message. Keep data/privacy declarations constant.

Use the experiment's sample-size/time estimate and declared outcome. Let tests include at least
a full week, and longer when more data is needed. Do not stop as soon as one variant is briefly
ahead or interpret a calendar deadline as significance. Check retention or available install
quality signals as well as acquisition. This can use Play Console reporting without adding
tracking to the games. See [experiment setup](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12053285?hl=en)
and [Google's experiment advice](https://google.play/business/store-listing-experiments/).

Priority order: warning cleanup and gameplay-first images; benefit-first full copy; clearer
game names; demand-led localization; then controlled experiments and video. There is currently
insufficient conversion evidence to attach credible percentage-uplift forecasts to these changes.
